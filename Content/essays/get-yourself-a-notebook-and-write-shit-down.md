I think it’s worth mentioning that a lot of you have a phobia for notebooks. Secondary school and university wasn’t fun for a lot of adults; thereby causing them to have grown a fear for pen and paper. They hate the thought of reading or writing.

The pen and notebook aren’t your enemies, they’re your friends.
You’re a blank page away from organizing your life. 
You’re a blank page away from documenting the life you’re currently living to pass on to people who come after you.
You’re a blank page away from planning your nearest future.

I’m not asking you to journal, I’m asking you to write down things. Everything. Funny enough, I’m directing this message specifically to the Nigerian man who fears that owning a pen and a notebook makes them look less masculine. Little do they know that the reason why they tend to forget things a lot is because they treat their brains like a storage bin. 

Our brains were never designed to keep things stored for a long period of time. You don’t have a terrible memory, you’re just using it wrongly. Your brain should have a backup the same way your computer does. The backup can be a notebook, the cloud or a hard drive.

You get the life you want by being intentional. Intentionally comes as a result of slowing down, noticing, documenting, distilling and planning. 

Start small. Get yourself a pocket notebook. Begin with a simple quote that you like. Let this notebook be a catchall for your everyday life and activities. 

As we get older, we tend to forget things easily. It’s often frustrating when the work you do on a daily basis requires you to remember small details frequently. And when your brain fails you which it most likely will, it leads to small mistakes with sometimes big consequences.

Owning a pocket notebook makes this avoidable because not only is it small enough to fit in your pocket but you can also carry it everywhere. It helps you hold information or details that you don’t want to forget. With a simple todo list, you can plan your day and map out your schedule to avoid wasting time. If you’ve never experienced the joy of bringing out a notebook during an argument to prove a point because you recorded it in the moment, then you’re missing a lot.

Having a pen and paper at all times helps you write constantly. You can record not just what was being said to you, but your feelings, smell, colors, descriptions, EVERYTHING. In time, you would have developed a personal system to help you write stuff faster and in a way that fits your life and work.

Every notebook records an event, every event is proof of life lived, and every life lived should be recorded.
