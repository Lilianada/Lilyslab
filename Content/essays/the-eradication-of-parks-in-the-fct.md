I came across this [page](https://www.manton.org/texas-state-parks/) from Manton's website where he records all the parks he has visited in the city of Texas. It made me ponder a bit on what we call a park here in Abuja. Texas state has about 90 parks and from the looks of it, these parks are beautiful natural terrains that haven't been commercialised. He attached images to all [32 parks](https://www.manton.org/2024/07/08/lunch-at-lbj.html) that he has visited and from all the images, you can see how green, calming and relaxing these parks are.

A little google search showed that there are about 185 parks and recreational gardens across the capital. But how many of these parks are open to the public?

What we call parks here are spaces where you find restaurants, bars, event centers and most of them lack greenry. You can decide to pick up a book and go to the park to read and relax but the experience will be a solid 2/10. It's either too noisy due to the music being played by bars and restaurants or you won't find open green areas to simply relax and unwind.

Least I forget, you also have to be security conscious when you're using these places because you might be susceptible to petty thives and kidnappers. Funny right? The park that's meant to be a safe space is also not shielded from the woes of insecurity that's plaguing this nation.

The lack of free natural spaces in the F.C.T and how the current minister is doing everything in his power to make sure that by the end of his tenure, all parks in Abuja have been privatized is just beyond me. Why sell spaces that gives joy to the youth? Jabi Lake Park has been sold off. Every weekend, walking through the lake park, you'd be amazed to see the number of people who come out every morning to get their bodies moving, engage in one sport or the other.

I love to walk, so when I find out that parks are been sold off to private entities who have no plans of improving on the parks for the public, it makes me even more resentful towards the government and its lack of sheer care for its citizens and their affairs.

BRING PARK FREE PARK!
