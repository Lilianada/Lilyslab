A while back I wrote a 500-word essay on [[why I write]], and I’ve been trying to improve my writing. I've tried to make my writing practice fun and interesting for the sake of consistency.

If, like me, you have an interest in learning to write better, then you might be interested to know that having a clear structure that guides you helps to make writing easier. I have devised a roadmap, a structure to follow. This is what works for me, and if you’re lost, you can try it and see if it sticks.

To write better, you must be willing to build a good writing habit. Those habits include writing every day, reading widely and being willing to have a bad first draft.

### Things I've learnt about writing

1. Disregard all you were taught in secondary school about writing.
2. Writing is hard work; be ready to write more to write better.
3. Aim to always have a rough first draft. A rough first draft is progress.
4. Do not edit as you write. Write when you write and edit at a later time.
5. Write like you talk.
6. Embrace brevity; trim your sentences and keep it them as simple as possible. Verlyn Klinkenborg wrote in his book, "Every word is optional until it proves essential". 
7. Never get attached to your first draft. Expect that 80% of your first draft will be weeded out. This will help you to make progress faster. 
8. Avoid the use of fancy words or technical jargon.
9. Use sentences of varying lengths.
10. Understand the usage of active and passive voice.
11. Redundancy isn't repetition. Know the difference between using redundant words e.g., 'collaborated together' and using repetition to build rhythm.
12. Trust that your reader is literate and intelligent. This way you don't need to hold their hands by using transition markers such as "however, therefore, finally" to get them to understand your points.
13. Avoid joining two complete sentences with only a comma (comma splice). If both halves could stand alone as sentences, use a period, a semicolon, or a joining word like “and” or “but.”
14. Understand subject-verb agreement and how to hyphenate two-word descriptions before a noun.
15.  Learn when to use a semicolon. A semicolon is used to join two connected independent clauses. The connecting sentences must be able to stand alone. 

### My Process

1. I pick a topic, question or idea that you're genuinely interested in. Having interest in a chosen idea helps to get you from first draft to finished work. 
2. Sit with my ideas for a couple of days before writing.
3. Make a rough first draft.
4. Keep it simple. The path to keeping my work simple comes from trimming and cutting things out completely. If a sentence can do without that extra word or phrase and still hold the same meaning, then cut it out.
5. Check for errors and make sure I am not repeating past mistakes.
6. Read the writing out loud. Doing this helps to give me a sense of how the work sounds in a conversation.
7. Publish.

I've made so many mistakes, but that's because I didn't know better. With the knowledge I have now, I strive to keep getting better.

I do have plans to write a book someday. I want to learn and improve how I write so that I have fewer things to worry about when I start that project.

I would love to get complimented on my writing, so I must get better. As I continue to learn and get better at writing, I'll keep on updating this essay.
