A while back I wrote a 500-word essay on [[why I write]], and I’ve been trying to improve my writing. I've tried to make my writing practice fun and interesting for the sake of consistency.

If, like me, you have an interest in learning to write better, then you might be interested to know that having a clear structure that guides you helps to make writing easier. I have devised a roadmap, a structure to follow. This is what works for me, and if you’re lost, you can try it and see if it sticks.

To write better, you must be willing to build a good writing habit. Those habits include writing every day, reading widely and being willing to have a bad first draft.

### Things I've learnt about writing

1. Disregard all you were taught in secondary school about writing.
2. Writing is hard work; be ready to write more to write better.
3. Aim to always have a rough first draft. A rough first draft is progress.
4. Do not edit as you write. Write when you write and edit at a later time.
5. Write like you talk.
6. Embrace brevity; trim your sentences and keep it them as simple as possible. Verlyn Klinkenborg wrote in his book, "Every word is optional until it proves essential". 
7. Never get attached to your first draft. Expect that 80% of your first draft will be weeded out. This will help you to make progress faster. 
8. Avoid the use of fancy words or technical jargon.
9. Use sentences of varying lengths.
10. Understand the usage of active and passive voice.
11. Redundancy isn't repetition. Know the difference between using redundant words e.g., 'collaborated together' and using repetition to build rhythm.
12. Trust that your reader is literate and intelligent. This way you don't need to hold their hands by using transition markers such as "however, therefore, finally" to get them to understand your points.
13. Avoid joining two complete sentences with only a comma (comma splice). If both halves could stand alone as sentences, use a period, a semicolon, or a joining word like “and” or “but.”
14. Understand subject-verb agreement and how to hyphenate two-word descriptions before a noun.
15.  Learn when to use a semicolon. A semicolon is used to join two connected independent clauses. The connecting sentences must be able to stand alone. 

### My Process

David Cleather in one of his recent articles, shared a "better process" that is worth checking out.[^1] 

1. I pick a topic, question or idea that I'm genuinely interested in. This is the essence for why I write so there has to be a main point. Having an interest in the chosen idea helps to get me from first draft to finished work. 

2. Have a concluding point. What exactly do I plan to achieve by writing the essay. "Writing Better" is the idea I've chosen to write on. At the end of the essay, I plan to also give others an idea of what writing better should typically entail and show them, through my own writing process how I have been able to improve my writing.

3. Sit with my ideas for a couple of days before writing. This step helps to get me to think about the idea, marinate on it and probably build arguments. I'm ready to write when I can go on a 5-minute rant about said idea to a friend. Other times, writing helps me think so I write to discover what I'm thinking.

4. Make a rough first draft and as quickly as possible. It's easier to make edits to a finished draft than finish an unfinished one. Write fast and write all you know. This isn't the time to correct mistakes or edit the body of work. I just regurgitate my thoughts onto paper. 

5. There are times when I have almost no idea what to write. That's the time when I do research. I watch videos, read articles or essays on the particular topic. I gather pointers that are simple, but able to get me to understand my topic/idea better. I collect lots of pointers from my research and these are usually the building block sof my finished work.

6. Keep it simple. The path to keeping my work simple comes from trimming and cutting things out completely. If a sentence can do without that extra word or phrase and still hold the same meaning, then cut it out.

7. Read the writing out loud. Doing this helps to give me a sense of how the work sounds in a conversation. It also helps step 8 as reading out loud is one of the best ways to catch errors.

8. Check for errors and make sure I am not repeating past mistakes.

9. Publish.


I would love to get complimented on my writing, so I must get better. As I continue to learn and get better at writing, I'll keep on updating this essay. [^1]: A better process is as follows: you collect all of the information as before. You then read and think. You think about what the information you are reading means to you. You search for other bits of literature that relate to the ideas that your reading provokes. What you are searching for is your key message, a story that you want to tell, or a position that you want to argue. This is not a retelling of the facts that you have read, but rather your own take on the topic. Your message should generally be able to be expressed as the essay title.

I've made so many mistakes, but that's because I didn't know better. With the knowledge I have now, I strive to keep getting better.

I do have plans to write a book someday. I want to learn and improve how I write so that I have fewer things to worry about when I start that project.
