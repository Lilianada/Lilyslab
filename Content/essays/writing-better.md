### Redundancy isn’t Repetition 

Bad example: "A fresh surge of adrenaline surges through me"

Triple repetition- “I am disappointed too in a way: disappointed that I have disappointed him” the colon in this sentence is doing the heavy lifting.
What comes after the colon is an explanation of what came before. “He lied to me that first day: lied so blatantly that I wondered who had taught him to lie so well.”

The building sentence - every letter creates a word, every word forms a phrase, every phrase becomes a sentence. Every sentence builds a paragraph. Every paragraph furnishes an argument.  

Epistrophe, ending every single phrase with the same word.
Anaphora- every clause in a sentence begins with the same word
“Every notebook records an event, every event is proof of life lived, and every life lived should be recorded.”

### Learning to Use a Semicolon 
If like me you have an interest in learning to write better, then you might be interested to know that using a semicolon shows how smart you are.

Examples of the usage:
- “I traveled to the village for my grandfather's funeral; it started on Thursday evening, and by Sunday evening, it was over.” - a semicolon is used to join two connected independent clauses together. Both sentences must be able to stand alone.
- The semicolon can be used before commonly used conjunction adverbs like however, nevertheless, and moreover.
- Semicolons are used to divide items within a list. For example, “I want to change my entire personal style; I’ll be doing this by buying new work shoes, running shoes and date-night shoes; gym outfits, work outfits and traditional attire.” The items within the lists in this example are closely related so the semicolon helps to separate them into categories.
