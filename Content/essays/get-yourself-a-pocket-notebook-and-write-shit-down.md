Our brains were never designed to keep things stored for a long period of time. Your brain should have a backup the same way your computer does. That backup can be a notebook, the cloud or a hard drive. 

For the sake of physical media and the trend of going analog in a digital world, that backup should be a notebook. A person who writes a lot is a person who thinks a lot. Writing requires you to think more, process events and form ideas. A 2014 study by Pam A. Mueller and Daniel M. Oppenheimer showed that writing your notes down by hand can improve "learning and retention".


### Why?

I started carrying a pocket notebook around last year and it really made me want to write. I was writing down random ass quotes I found online, recording fleeting thoughts, and making life plans.

When you start carrying a notebook around, I promise you start to notice things. From the smell the wind carries, to how the trees dance, something smart that someone said, a new word you heard and so on. What is life without being able to notice things?

I know you have a notes app on your phone, but there's a distinct difference in experience. Writing things down on paper requires you to slow down the pace of your thoughts and see things clearly.

Each page you've written is a reflection of a life you've lived.


### Start small. 

Get yourself a pocket notebook. They are perfect for capturing quick notes, sketches, and ideas the moment they strike. Let this notebook be a catchall for your everyday life and your constant companion for spontaneity. 

As we get older, we tend to forget things easily. It’s often frustrating when the work you do on a daily basis requires you to remember small details frequently. And when your brain fails you, which it most likely will, it leads to small mistakes with oftentimes big consequences.

Owning a pocket notebook makes this avoidable because it is small enough to fit in your pocket. It helps you hold information or details that you don’t want to forget. With a simple todo list, you can plan your day and map out your schedule to avoid wasting time. 

Having a pen and paper at all times helps you write constantly. You can record not just what is said to you, but your feelings, smells, colors, descriptions, EVERYTHING. As time progresses, you begin to develop a personal system to help you write stuff faster and in a way that fits your life and the work you do.


### How to use the pocket notebook effectively

Here's a simple system that I came across after watching [*Lilla Björn's video*](https://youtu.be/6Hm-mUNYfz8?si=e58S4MD-T_cSuV8a) on how to get your attention span back. This system will reduce the friction that keeps you from writing. 

When you write, you use a lot of brain power because you have to slow down and articulate your thoughts enough to put them down on paper. When you add an extra task or two like editing and organizing, it creates friction that not only tires you out, but makes the process much more stressful. You shouldn't be writing, editing and organizing at the same time. 

Writing should be simple and fun. So adopt this system for ultimate productivity:

1. **Collect and capture everything:** Use your pocket notebook as a catchall for thoughts, plans, tasks, reminders and things you want to remember for later. The goal is to offload onto the page as much as you can. Your notebook becomes an external storage that allows you to free up space in your head and think better.
2. **Filter all you've collected:** Once a day or at least once a week, take a few minutes to review your entries. Cross out completed tasks and unimportant thoughts. This review helps you to filter out what needs attention and what is fleeting.
3. **Migrate, Organize and Elaborate:** The important things left from your review should now be moved into your main planner, notes or laptop. Organize those entries based on their categories. I, for example, move all ideas I have for an essay into my digital garden where I am able to flesh out my points and build the essays. I move some entries into my [[commonplace journal]] and others to my daily journal.
4. **Publish or Archive:** This is the last step in this system. I publish my finished works or simply archive them for later.
  

### Why It Works:

- By having a dedicated place for collecting all such information, you spend less time contemplating where to store what.
- Thinking on paper (what writing is) is an excellent tool for generating ideas, solving problems and gaining insights. The pocket notebook forces you to stay offline and engage in a more focused thinking process. You are able to avoid distraction from notifications and doomscrolling.

Every blank page in your notebook is a thought waiting to be captured, an idea longing to be born, an observation anticipating discovery, a conversation between who you are and who you're becoming. You get the life you want by being intentional; slowing down, noticing, documenting, and planning. And because your notebook is an extension of your mind, it helps you keep a record of your life and draws you to make serendipitous connections between concepts and ideas.

Every notebook records an event; every event is proof of life lived and every life lived should be recorded.
