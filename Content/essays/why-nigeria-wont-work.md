A working Nigeria means a lower cost of living, good roads, affordable housing and constant power supply; standard regulations for food safety and a good healthcare system; quality and subsidized education without debt alongside good-paying jobs and a favourable labour market; free and fair elections and accountability on the part of the leaders; subsidized petrol and fuel prices, security against terrorist groups and fair policies for businesses to thrive.

I'm not big on politics. In fact, I dislike politics because it is being treated as a game and the games the politicians play jeopardize not only my future but that of millions of Nigerians.

We all want our country to get better, but is that really achievable? So much of how we do things and how we behave is truly terrible. As a people, we're poor, greedy, selfish and wicked. And power in the hands of a poor and greedy man is simply destruction waiting to happen. 

I've had the misfortune of witnessing the rot that has deeply infested the government institutions that are meant to protect us. When ordinary people are given a little power, they become monsters that are hungry to control, exploit and destroy. Because tell me why policemen will raid a student lodge in a school environment at 1am, pick up any male student they find hanging around and take them to a location that is 100% not the police station? It doesn't end there. They go further to demand that the students pay money before they are released or else they'll be taken to the police cell and detained for no just cause.

Our problems aren't caused only by the people at the top as you can see. They're also caused by all of us at the grassroots. Everyone can do it better than the people at the top, until they get to the top. Visit any small government parastatal and you'll see on a smaller scale, the same rot that goes on in the bigger seats. Everyone is thinking about their own pockets; they only understand the language of money, more money and tribe. And you wonder why tribalism and money are weapons being used by a defective government to blind us from seeing clearly the path of destruction that we're on. 

The problem isn't that you're not a good person who could be a good leader. The problem is that bad company corrupts good people. I mean, if you lie down with dogs, there's a very high chance that you wake up with fleas. 

Some days I wish we could clear out every leader and start over. But they're all somebody's daddy and mummy, so I guess not. And even if they were all gone, who's to say the next set of people who get into those seats won't be worse?

I need you to take a good look at the current clamor for who should become the next President of Nigeria. Honest Nigerians want a change in government. They want to vote in someone who cares, is smart and able to lead the country down the right path. But we've seen this before; we know how it all pans out. We've come out in our numbers to vote, but at the end of the day, our votes didn't count. It won't be the first time, but how can we make it the last? Let me know when and if you get an answer to that.

As much as we all want change, the possibility of us witnessing that change in our lifetime is very slim. We can do our best, but our best might never be enough.
