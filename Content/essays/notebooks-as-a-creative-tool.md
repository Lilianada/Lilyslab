A 2014 study by by Pam A. Mueller and Daniel M. Oppenheimer showed that writing your notes down by hand can improve "learning and retention". The notebook is a creative tool used in training your mind for deep focus and flow. They don't just hold words, they hold identity.

*…we write because we’re human, and writing is a way of seeing and feeling and hearing, a way of asking and knowing, a way of creating and making sense, of expressing and communicating. Maja Wilson, Reimagining Writing Assessment.*

A notebook is more than just paper. It's an open space for discovery and innovation. Each blank page is an invitation to explore, experiment, and create without limits. It's where unfinished thoughts and raw ideas can safely take root, capturing growth, tracking progress, and telling the story of your creative journey. 

I've gathered a list of famous creatives who own and use a notebook religiously in their daily life and creative work. This is to show you that the notebook is a tool for creativity and not the creative medium. A lot of creatives begin their creative process with a notebook. It is a way for them to collect creative raw materials, think without pressure and find themselves.

### Collectors 

George Lucas carries a pocket notebook with him everywhere he goes. He uses this to collect pieces he needs and use for his movies; to write down any character names he thinks are interesting and any scene he wants to remember. Like George Lucas, use a pocket notebook to never lose any of your ideas.

Bob Dylan uses particularly a pocket notebook and treats it as his first step in the creative process. He writes down phrases he overhears, makes a chain of rhymes, and jots down drafts of lyrics. He basically uses his pocket notebook as a catchall for his creative juices.

Anthony Bourdain carried with him three to four moleskin notebook as an essential travel item. He does all his writing by hand first before he transfers and edits them as he types on his laptop.

### Thinkers

Hayao Miyazaki uses his secret notebook to think on paper. One reason why it's secret is because it's not finished. There's no finished work that goes on within his notebooks. His notebook is just a starting place for his thinking. There's no pressure that he puts on it to develop anything within it. It's more like a personal diary to put thoughts down onto paper.

Wes Anderson carries a small notebook in his pocket to capture ideas whenever they come. He uses seven notebooks for every singular movie. The first one to three notebooks get filled with just a lot of ideas. Then in notebooks four and five, he starts to flesh out scenes, flesh out characters, different shots he wants to do. And then in notebooks six and seven, he'll come together with a complete movie and script off of those notebooks. When you look at the actual pages of these notebooks, what's really interesting is that they're not linear. They're spatial, and that's what makes them so vital to his process. He fills the pages with dialogue, characters, scenes, and visual details.

Octavia Butler keeps notebooks filled with personal reflections, story ideas, goals and affirmations. 

James Baldwin writes by hand in orange notebooks and on yellow legal ads, filling pages with observations, ideas, and early drafts. he uses writing to confront truths about himself and the world, even the ones he'd rather avoid.

### People Finding Themselves

Frida Khalo keeps notebooks where she writes in brightly colored inks, mixes writing with watercolor paintings, and assigns personal meanings to different colors. Her notebooks is a mix of writing, sketches and self-portraits, poems, dreams, and reflections. She writes about her love interests, childhood memories, political beliefs, and physical pain. She uses her notebook as a way explore feelings and thoughts she doesn't always express in her paintings.

Solange Knowles started keeping a journal as a child, filling it with personal thoughts and poetry. In her teenage years, she started journaling about her feelings, everyday experiences, and discovering her creative voice. She uses writing to explore who she is, where she comes from, and who she's becoming. She moves between personal essays, poetry, and songwriting. 

### What kind of notebook-keeper are you? Or which one do you want to become? 

I, for one, use a notebook to understand myself better. That’s what I meant when I said notebooks hold identity. I try to declutter my brain a lot, and I have several notebooks for different things that aid this process. I keep a catchall journal, a daily journal, two commonplace journals, a reading journal and an art journal. I want to live a creative life, so I adopt creative practices that fuel my goal.

If you're not one to use a notebook, but you aim to also do creative work and live creatively, like the creatives mentioned above, you should [[get yourself a notebook and write shit down]].
