A few thoughts on tightening this up:

**Your strongest elements:**
- The philosophy of "small sites over massive platforms" resonates with a real movement happening right now
- The template approach with explicit permission to copy/modify is smart - removes the blank page problem
- The community aspect around sharing and iterating on designs creates natural engagement

**Areas that could use more clarity:**
- How exactly does someone go from inspiration to having their actual site live? Is Minispace hosting these sites, or teaching people to deploy elsewhere?
	- Teaching them to deploy elsewhere
- The revenue model isn't clear - you mention wanting it to be cost-effective but not what makes it sustainable.
	- No revenue, but support from members or visitors is welcomed.
- The scope feels quite broad (templates, tutorials, community, directory, hosting?) - might be worth identifying the core MVP
	- Templates, directory and tutorials. community comes when people actually find it. No hosting.

**Questions to help focus:**
- What's the one thing someone can do on Minispace in month one that they can't do anywhere else?
	- Everything aly exist can't think of a uvp.
- Are you primarily competing with Squarespace/Wix (ease of use) or with dev communities like IndieWeb (philosophy and inspiration)?
		- Do not pan or aim to compete with anyone.
- What would make someone choose to build their site through Minispace versus just following a tutorial on their own?
	- The reason that makes people do stuff i guess. You dfind inspiration here, you see how to do it, you're provided with resources to make the work 80% easy, the remaining 20% is to channel your creativity and technical thinking.

The manifesto and philosophy feel authentic to you. The question now is finding the sharpest way to execute on that vision without trying to solve everything at once.
