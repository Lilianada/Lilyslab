My garden is a clean space—minimal, free from feature bloating, easy to navigate, simple and personal, allowing me to 

link my contents. I didn't know how good this was until I started linking my content. Writing one note, reading it, and seeing new topics to write on within it because of bi-directional linking.

Owning your own space on the web allows you to create more content than you consume passively. You can create content that stands the test of time—evergreen content—as you keep tending to it from time to time. It allows you to customize as much as possible and own your data instead of feeding it to big corporations that sell it. No pressure to create or perform, no ads constantly in your face. You can make meaningful contributions and engage freely without restrictions.

While there are many tools I could use to bring this to life, I chose to create something for myself, one that I can customize to my preference, one that I can design and update without restrictions and as time goes on. 

This is why I created Braindump.

I do not have to dance to the tools of algorithms or care about optimizations, viral hooks, unwanted and annoying ads (I hate this one heavily), or trending sounds. I have the ability to edit and update as I like without paying subscriptions for certain features or giving my data to several apps because they all have different features I want.

> Optimization makes even the most sloppish content go viral.
